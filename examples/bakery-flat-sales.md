# Example: a business problem

**User:** box My small bakery's weekend sales have been flat for six months. I have two staff, one small oven, and about HKD 3,000 a month I could spend on trying new things.

**think_out_of_the_box:**

**Problem:** Weekend sales have been flat for six months, and you want to grow them with a small oven, two staff and about HKD 3,000 a month.
**Limits I used:** 2 staff, 1 small oven, HKD 3,000 a month to experiment with. I assumed a walk-in shop in Hong Kong, which you didn't say.

## Obvious answers
What most AI replies would say. I've listed them on purpose, and some may be exactly right.
1. **Post on Instagram or Xiaohongshu** (reels of the bake, product photos). This is still the right call if you aren't posting at least 3 times a week.
2. **Weekend-only special item.** This still works if your regulars say they're bored of the range.
3. **Bundles or discounts** (a box of 4, a late-day markdown). It's the right call if you throw away stock at close.
4. **Delivery apps** (Foodpanda, Deliveroo). It's right if your location has low foot traffic.
5. **Pre-order for weekend pickup.** It's right if you sell out early and guess quantities.
6. **Stamp card or loyalty scheme.** It's right if you already have many repeat customers.
7. **Partner with nearby cafés, offices or schools.** It's right if you have a spare hour on a weekday to pitch.
8. **Ask customers what they want** (a survey or a counter question). It's right if you haven't asked in months. There are more ideas of the same kind.

## Hidden assumptions
- The problem is too few people walking in on weekends, not what each person spends or when they arrive.
- Everything is baked in your oven and sold fresh on the same day.
- Customers drift in evenly through opening hours and find everything available.
- Growth means adding something: a product, a channel or a promotion.
- Price only goes down in tests (discounts), never up.

## Answers out of the box
### 1. Stretch: Raise the price, don't cut it
**Idea:** Put your top two sellers up by HKD 3–5 for two weekends. Leave everything else alone. Compare units sold and total revenue per item with the previous two weekends.
**Move used:** Invert. Instead of discounting to attract buyers, test whether the same buyers will pay more.
**Assumption broken:** Growth needs more customers, and price only goes down.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #3. **How this differs:** it raises revenue per sale for the customers you already have, rather than trading margin for volume.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Change two price tags on Saturday and note units sold per hour. Cost is HKD 0.
**Biggest risk:** Regulars may notice and leave. Pick items that aren't your signature product.

### 2. Leap: Sell unbaked, so the customer's appliance does the baking
**Idea:** Sell frozen unbaked cookie dough balls or scones, with a printed card for air-fryer and oven times. Make them on a weekday when the oven is idle, and sell them at the counter or by pre-order. Your oven stops being the limit on weekend sales.
**Move used:** Remove the biggest constraint. It takes the one small oven out of the bottleneck.
**Assumption broken:** Everything is baked in your oven and sold fresh on the day.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #4. **How this differs:** you aren't adding a way to deliver the same product. The customer finishes the bake, and you sell on days and at times you're closed.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Ask the next 20 weekend customers "Do you have an oven or air fryer?" and "Would you buy 6 frozen dough balls for HKD 60?" If 6 or more say yes, bake one batch of 15 packs, which is about HKD 250 in ingredients.
**Biggest risk:** Many Hong Kong flats have no oven, so the question above could kill it quickly. You also need freezer space.

### 3. Weird but testable: Sell a "showtime", not a shelf
**Idea:** Choose one item and announce that it comes out of the oven once each weekend day at a fixed minute, for example 11:20. Write the time on a chalkboard outside, and put it in your Instagram bio. Let a small queue form at the door.
**Move used:** Borrow from theatre. A fixed performance time pulls a crowd into one moment, and the crowd draws passers-by.
**Assumption broken:** Customers arrive evenly through the day and find everything available.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #2. **How this differs:** the product stays the same. What changes is that customers arrive together, and the queue is visible to people walking past.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Run it for two weekends with a HKD 30 chalkboard and one Instagram story. Count the people at the door at showtime and the sales in the 30 minutes after.
**Biggest risk:** The batch ties up your only oven, and customers who arrive late may leave annoyed. Keep it to one small batch.

**Filtered:** 2 candidates dropped (an early hiker-hours opening that depends on a location I don't know, and a "closed when gone" policy that risks losing regulars), 3 moved to the obvious list (a guest baker from your customers, a baking workshop after close, and a late-day mystery bag).
**Honesty:** these are not among the obvious answers above. That does not mean nobody has tried them.
