# Example: a personal problem

**User:** box I just moved to a new city for work and I don't know anyone. I'm shy, I work 9 to 6, and I have about HKD 400 a month to spend on a social life.

**think_out_of_the_box:**

**Problem:** You're new in the city, don't know anyone, and are shy. You want a social life that fits a 9-to-6 job and about HKD 400 a month.
**Limits I used:** 9 to 6 work, about HKD 400 a month, shy. You didn't name the city, so I assumed Hong Kong because of the HKD.

## Obvious answers
What most AI replies would say. They're listed on purpose, and some may be exactly right.
1. Join one recurring class or club (climbing, language exchange, run club). Still the right call when you want the same faces every week.
2. Go to Meetup or Eventbrite events. Still right for sampling many groups before committing.
3. Say yes to colleagues' lunches and after-work drinks. Still right if your office has people your age.
4. Volunteer weekly. Still right if you want a shared purpose and no entry fee.
5. Use friend-finding apps (Bumble BFF and similar). Still right if you're more comfortable typing first.
6. Join a hiking or sports group. Still right because you're side by side, not face to face.
7. Host your own small recurring event, such as a weekly walk or board-game night. Still right once you have two or three contacts.
8. Fix one evening a week as "social night" so it survives a busy week.

And more of the same kind.

## Hidden assumptions
- Friends are made in a room full of strangers.
- Social life happens after 6, so free evenings are the only resource.
- Shyness is something you have to get past before you can make friends.
- The HKD 400 has to be spent on the social activity itself.
- As the new person, you're the one asking for something.

## Answers out of the box
### 1. Stretch: Ask in writing, meet the repliers
**Idea:** Find 10 people who post about something you like (a hike, a food scene, a hobby). Send each a short message: "I just moved here. What's the one place you'd take a visitor?" Meet only the one or two who reply warmly, for a single coffee or walk.
**Move used:** Subtract. It removes the live crowd and the instant small talk.
**Assumption broken:** Friends are made in a room full of strangers.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #5 (apps), and **how this differs:** you pick people by a stated interest and ask for a recommendation, not friendship, so the replies screen for you.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Send 10 messages over three evenings. Cost: HKD 0.
**Biggest risk:** Reply rates may be low, and cold messages can read as spam if they're vague.

### 2. Leap: Use the 9 hours, not the evenings
**Idea:** Ask one colleague a week from a different team for a 20-minute coffee, framed as "I'd like to understand what your team does". Work gives you the agenda, so you don't need small talk. Keep the ones who are easy to talk to and invite them to something outside work later.
**Move used:** Solve a different problem. The goal becomes "become known to people", and your workday already has the people.
**Assumption broken:** Social life happens after 6, so free evenings are the only resource.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #3 (colleague drinks), and **how this differs:** it's a work-purpose meeting in work hours with a built-in topic. It doesn't depend on an evening invite or on a social vibe.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Ask two colleagues this week. Cost: roughly HKD 60 for two coffees, if you pay for them.
**Biggest risk:** The friendships may stay work-only, and it depends on how friendly your office is.

### 3. Weird but testable: Go as the photographer
**Idea:** Message one group organizer (run club, hobby meetup): "I'm new. Can I take free photos at your next session?" At the session, your job is the camera. Ask each person "Where should I send yours?", and the photo gives you a reason to message them afterwards.
**Move used:** Borrow from a distant field. Event and press photographers get access and an excuse to approach people through the role.
**Assumption broken:** Shyness is something you have to get past before you can make friends.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #4 (volunteering), and **how this differs:** the role produces an object, the photo, that gives you a reason to get contacts and write to people afterwards. You don't have to open conversations cold.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Send one DM to one organizer, then attend one session. Cost: HKD 0 to a session fee, within your budget.
**Biggest risk:** The organizer may say no, and you must ask people's permission before posting any photo.
