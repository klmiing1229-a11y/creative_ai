**Problem:** You start studying hard, drop off around day 4, and want a way to keep going.
**Limits I used:** About 2 free hours a day, a part-time job, and no money.

## Obvious answers
These are what most AI replies would say. I've listed them on purpose, and some may be exactly right for you.
1. **Fixed daily slot, same place, tied to something you already do.** This is still the right call if your job shifts are regular.
2. **Shrink the goal to a 10-20 minute minimum.** It's still right if you skip because starting feels heavy.
3. **Pomodoro or timed blocks.** It's still right if you sit down but drift.
4. **Habit tracker or streak chain.** It's still right if seeing a chain motivates you.
5. **Study buddy or accountability partner.** It's still right if you respond to other people.
6. **Phone out of the room or a site blocker.** It's still right if the phone is where the time goes.
7. **Planned rest days and a "never miss twice" restart rule.** It's still right if one missed day turns into quitting.
8. **Stop mid-task so tomorrow's start is easy.** It's still right if the hard part is the first five minutes.

And more of the same kind: weekly review, rewards, goal-setting.

## Hidden assumptions
- The day-4 drop is a willpower problem, not a novelty problem.
- Consistency means the same amount every day, and a streak is the unit.
- Studying is private, with no audience until the exam.
- Your 2 hours go to one subject, in one place, in one way.
- The fix has to be a new rule, not a change to what or where you study.

## Answers out of the box
### 1. Stretch: Crop rotation
**Idea:** Treat your three-day burst as the unit instead of fighting it. Every third day, change the course, the place, or the method (reading, practice questions, writing summaries). Farmers rotate crops because the same crop depletes the soil. You rotate before your interest runs out.
**Move used:** Borrow from a distant field (farming). It turns the day-4 drop into a timer you plan around.
**Assumption broken:** The day-4 drop is a willpower problem, not a novelty problem.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #7, and **how this differs:** you don't restart after you fail. You change what you're doing on a fixed 3-day clock, before interest fades.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Days 1-3 on course A at your desk. Days 4-6 on course B somewhere else, such as a library or empty classroom. Day 7 off. Cost: $0.
**Biggest risk:** Switching courses can hurt a course with an imminent deadline that needs steady daily work.

### 2. Leap: Become the teacher
**Idea:** Change the goal from "study 2 hours a day" to "give one 20-minute explanation to 2-3 classmates every Sunday." Ask them for the slot and tell them the topic in advance. You study during the week because you'd be embarrassed to show up unprepared, and the lesson is the deliverable.
**Move used:** Give it to the opposite person. You go from being the student to being the one people wait on.
**Assumption broken:** Studying is private, with no audience until the exam.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #5, and **how this differs:** nobody checks on you. Others receive something from you on a set date, so the cost of skipping is a bad lesson, not an awkward text.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Message 2 classmates today: "Can I explain [topic] to you for 20 min on Sunday?" Cost: $0.
**Biggest risk:** Classmates may flake or say no, and you could teach something wrong with nobody to catch it.

### 3. Weird but testable: Let a die decide
**Idea:** Each day, before you think about it, roll a die. 1-2 means a full 2 hours. 3-4 means 45 minutes. 5 means just skim headings for 10 minutes. 6 means a guaranteed day off. Every roll counts as a pass, so there is no streak to break. Pause the dice in exam week and study normally.
**Move used:** Add an absurd constraint (no decisions allowed), borrowing the unpredictability of a slot machine.
**Assumption broken:** Consistency means the same amount every day, and a streak is the unit.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #7, and **how this differs:** nothing is planned as a failure and no minimum is set. The die makes the decision, and every outcome is valid, so nothing is left to quit.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Use a phone dice app for 7 days and write the roll and what you did on paper. Cost: $0.
**Biggest risk:** You might roll low several days running and cover too little. Check the total hours after the week.
