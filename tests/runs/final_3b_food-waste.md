5. **Cut the menu and reuse ingredients across dishes.** Still the right call if many ingredients are bought for only one dish.

## Answers out of the box
### 1. Stretch: Pre-sell tomorrow's special
**Idea:** Each afternoon, message your regulars on WhatsApp and put a chalkboard in the restaurant. Offer one special for tomorrow, reserved by 3pm. You prep that dish only for the reservations plus 2 or 3 spares.
**Move used:** Make the problem disappear. If demand is known before you prep, there is nothing to guess.
**Assumption broken:** The kitchen must guess demand before it knows who will order.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #2, and **how this differs:** customers commit to the dish in advance, so you aren't forecasting from past sales.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Run it for 5 days on one dish, with a message to your 10 or so most regular customers. Cost: HKD 0.
**Biggest risk:** You may have too few regulars on WhatsApp for the numbers to mean anything.

### 2. Leap: Sell the scraps as a product
**Idea:** Trimmings and ingredients about to go off become something with a long shelf life, such as chilli oil, pickles or stock concentrate. You sell it in small jars at the till. Waste becomes a small product line.
**Move used:** Solve a different problem. The goal changes from "less waste" to "waste that earns money".
**Assumption broken:** Waste is a cost to shrink, not something you could sell.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #6, and **how this differs:** the product is separate from the day's service and keeps for weeks, so it doesn't need to sell that night.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Make one batch from this week's trimmings and fill about 12 small jars (roughly HKD 60). Give free tastes to regulars and ask who would pay HKD 30.
**Biggest risk:** I don't know whether your licence allows selling packaged food, so check that before selling anything.

### 3. Weird but testable: Hand the HKD 500 to whoever scrapes the plates
**Idea:** Let the dishwasher or closing person set tomorrow's prep numbers on a card each night. They see exactly what comes back and what goes in the bin. The HKD 500 is their budget to spend on anything that cuts waste.
**Move used:** Give it to the opposite person. The decision moves to the person at the end of the chain, not the start.
**Assumption broken:** The cook or owner makes the decisions that cause waste.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #2, and **how this differs:** the person who sees the waste decides the quantities, instead of the cook working from sales history.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Try it for 5 nights, with your own numbers kept to compare. Cost: HKD 0, since you only commit the money if it works.
**Biggest risk:** Your dishwasher may not want the job, or may under-prep and lose you sales.
