# Why you stop after 3 days

Starting strong for three days and then stopping is a common pattern. It usually means the plan was too big, not that you lack discipline. Day 1 you plan 2 hours of perfect studying. By day 3 you're tired, you miss one session, and "I've ruined the streak" turns into quitting. So the fix is a smaller plan that survives bad days, not more motivation.

## Ideas

1. **Set a minimum of 20 minutes.** On a bad day, you do 20 minutes and stop. That counts as a win and keeps the streak alive. Most days you'll keep going anyway, because starting is the hard part.
2. **Plan 90 minutes, not 120.** Two hours scheduled with a job on top leaves no slack. The extra 30 minutes is your buffer.
3. **Fix the trigger, not the time.** "Study at 7pm" fails when your shift runs late. Use an anchor like "after I eat dinner, I open the notes before I touch my phone." Do it in the same spot every day.
4. **Decide tomorrow's first task tonight.** Write one line, like "[course]: redo quiz problems 1–4." Most of the friction is deciding what to do, not doing it.
5. **Study by doing, not rereading.** Quiz yourself, redo past problems, and explain a concept out loud. It feels harder, but it's faster, and the difficulty tells you you're learning.
6. **Allow a one-day miss, never two.** Miss a day and you do your 20-minute minimum the next day, no guilt. The streak only breaks if you skip twice in a row.
7. **Plan around your job.** Heavy subjects go on days you're fresh. Light work (reviewing notes, flashcards) goes on work days. You can also use commute or break time for 10 minutes of flashcards.
8. **Use free tools.** A paper or phone checklist, your phone's free timer (25 minutes on, 5 off), the library, and a study buddy for a weekly check-in all cost nothing.

## Action plan

**This week: make it too easy to fail**
- **Day 1 (today):** Pick your anchor (the trigger from idea 3) and a fixed spot. Write tomorrow's single task. Do 20 minutes tonight.
- **Days 2–7:** Do 30–45 minutes only. Don't go longer even if you feel great. The goal is to avoid the day-3 crash by not burning your energy early.
- Tick a box on paper each day. Don't track anything else.

**Weeks 2–3: build up**
- Raise to 60 minutes on most days. Keep the 20-minute minimum for bad days.
- Add one retrieval block per session: 10 minutes of quizzing yourself on yesterday's material.
- Pick one fixed rest day, so it's planned and not a failure.

**Week 4 onward: full 90 minutes**
- Rotate subjects by day so you aren't facing everything at once.
- Every Sunday, spend 10 minutes on a review. Ask what you did, which day broke, and what the one fix is. Change only that one thing.

## If you stop anyway

Expect it to happen at some point, and have a restart rule ready: **"Do 20 minutes today. Don't make up for missed days."** Trying to catch up on missed hours is what turns one lapse into a three-week gap.
