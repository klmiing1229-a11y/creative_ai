**Problem:** Get 10 paying customers for a short-video service aimed at small shops, using about 10 hours a week and HKD 500.
**Limits I used:** 10 free hours a week, HKD 500, university student. I assumed Hong Kong shops and that you already have a phone and basic editing skills. You didn't state either.

## Obvious answers
What most AI replies would say. Listed on purpose, and some may be exactly right.
1. Build a small portfolio of 3 sample videos, still the right call when you have nothing to show.
2. Offer a free or heavily discounted first video, still the right call when shops don't trust you yet.
3. Cold DM or email shops on Instagram and Xiaohongshu, still the right call because it's free and your time is the only cost.
4. Walk into nearby shops and pitch the owner in person, still the right call for street-level shops that aren't active online.
5. Post your own work on Instagram, TikTok and Xiaohongshu so shops find you, still the right call as a slow background channel.
6. Ask each happy customer for referrals and testimonials, still the right call once you have your first 2 or 3.
7. Sell fixed packages at a clear price (e.g. 3 videos for HKD X), still the right call because it makes the decision easy.
8. Join local business groups and shop-owner communities, still the right call if you can find active ones.

And more of the same kind.

## Hidden assumptions
- You must find shops that need video. The shop doesn't have to be the one asking.
- The shop owner is the buyer. Someone else could pay for the video.
- You pitch first and make the video after. The order could be reversed.
- "Customer" means a shop that pays HKD for a video package. Other kinds of payer or payment could count.
- HKD 500 is for advertising or tools. It could go into the product itself.

## Answers out of the box

### 1. Stretch: Make the video first, then sell it
**Idea:** Pick 20 shops with weak or no video. Film a 15-second clip of each one on your phone in about 20 minutes. Send each owner their own finished clip with one line: "This is yours. HKD 300 to use it, or delete it." Nothing is shown to shops you haven't already filmed.
**Move used:** Invert it. Normally you sell the video and then make it. Here you make it first and sell it second.
**Assumption broken:** You pitch first and make the video after.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #2 (free first video), and **how this differs:** the owner doesn't have to say yes before work starts, so they react to a real video of their own shop instead of an offer.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Film 5 shops over one weekend and send each owner their clip. The cost is about HKD 0 plus transport, and the signal is how many reply or pay.
**Biggest risk:** Some owners may be annoyed that you filmed their shop without asking, and 20 minutes of unpaid work per shop adds up if few reply.

### 2. Leap: Sell to the landlord or the building, not the shop
**Idea:** Contact the management office or landlord of a small-shop cluster, such as an old mall, a market floor or a street of 30 stalls. Offer to make a 60-second "visit this floor" video plus a 10-second clip for each tenant, paid as a single deal. One yes gives you many shops and one invoice.
**Move used:** Give it to the opposite person. Instead of persuading shops one at a time, you pitch the party that wants all of them to look busy.
**Assumption broken:** The shop owner is the buyer.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #4 (walk in and pitch), and **how this differs:** the buyer is the building and not the shop, and one decision brings in the whole group of tenants.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Email or visit 5 management offices with one sample clip and a flat price. Cost is about HKD 0 to 50 for travel and printing.
**Biggest risk:** Management offices may not have a marketing budget, or may take weeks to decide, which breaks the under-a-week test.

### 3. Weird but testable: Take payment in trade or foot traffic, then convert
**Idea:** Offer shops a video in exchange for a coupon or voucher you can redeem, such as a coffee, a haircut or a meal. Do 3 such trades to get a real clip, a real reaction and a real before/after number (e.g. views). Then go back to each owner and ask to be paid in cash for the next one.
**Move used:** Solve a different problem. The goal is no longer cash from the first 10 shops. It is proof from the first 10 shops, with cash as the second step.
**Assumption broken:** "Customer" means a shop that pays HKD for a video package.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #2 (free first video), and **how this differs:** you get something back and you set a conversion date up front, so the trade is the start of a paid relationship and not a giveaway.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Propose a trade to 5 food or service shops and see whether 2 accept. Cost is about HKD 0 up front and you redeem the vouchers yourself.
**Biggest risk:** Shops may value the video at a coupon price forever, and it could be hard to move them to cash.
