**Problem:** You start studying strong, quit after about three days, and want a way to keep going.
**Limits I used:** About 2 free hours a day, a part-time job, and no money.

## Obvious answers
What most AI replies would say. Listed on purpose; some may be exactly right.
1. **Fixed daily slot tied to your shift pattern.** Still right if your job hours are stable.
2. **Streak calendar or habit tracker.** Still right if seeing a chain of ticks motivates you.
3. **Start much smaller (20 minutes, or just open the book).** Still right if each session feels heavy before you begin.
4. **Pomodoro blocks with the phone in another room.** Still right if focus inside the session is the problem, not showing up.
5. **Study partner or accountability buddy.** Still right if you have one reliable person.
6. **Reward after each session.** Still right if you respond to small treats.
7. **Planned rest days and a "never miss twice" restart rule.** Still right if you restart on your own after a gap.
8. **Practice questions and teach-back instead of rereading.** Still right if you are rereading and not remembering.

...and more of the same kind.

## Hidden assumptions
- A steady, identical routine is the goal, and novelty is a distraction.
- Studying means working through material alone, for hours.
- The plan has to be a dated sequence (day 1, day 2, day 3...).
- Your own free time is the only resource you can use.
- Stopping at day 3 or 4 is a discipline failure, not something the plan produces.

## Answers out of the box
### 1. Stretch: Three-day seasons
**Idea:** Your habit is already "start strong for three days". Plan for that. Each 3-day block gets its own topic, place or method, and ends with a 10-minute self-test. Day 4 opens a new block, so the novelty that fuels days 1 to 3 is supplied again.
**Move used:** Borrow from a distant field. TV and games run in seasons and levels, not one endless episode.
**Assumption broken:** A steady, identical routine is the goal.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #7, and **how this differs:** #7 rests and then restarts the same material by willpower. This resupplies the novelty on a schedule, so there is no lapse to recover from.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Run two blocks over six days. Block A is topic X at your desk. Block B is topic Y in a library or café. Count the sessions done. Cost: 0.
**Biggest risk:** Topics can end up half-covered, and novelty may wear off by block 3 or 4.

### 2. Leap: Book the office hour
**Idea:** Email a lecturer or TA and book one free office-hour slot this week. Study becomes finding the three questions you can't answer by then. Staff time is free and usually unused, and the appointment is a fixed point you can't quietly skip.
**Move used:** Remove the biggest constraint. A fee or tutor would force you to show up, so get that force without paying.
**Assumption broken:** Your own free time is the only resource, and you work alone.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #5, and **how this differs:** The other person isn't tracking your consistency. They are an expert who rewards prepared questions, and the unit of work changes from hours to questions.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Send one email today asking for a slot within 5 days. Bring 3 written questions. Cost: 0.
**Biggest risk:** It assumes the course has staff who are reachable.

### 3. Weird but testable: The jar, no calendar
**Idea:** Write 15 small tasks on paper slips, such as "do 5 questions on topic X" or "summarise one page". Put them in a jar. In each session, draw one and finish it. There is no day count and no plan to fall behind on, so a missed day leaves no backlog to feel guilty about.
**Move used:** Subtract. Remove the dated sequence that turns one missed day into "I've failed".
**Assumption broken:** The plan has to be a dated sequence.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #2, and **how this differs:** A tracker makes gaps visible. This removes the thing a gap can be measured against.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Make 15 slips from paper you already have. Draw one per session for 7 days and count the sessions. Cost: 0.
**Biggest risk:** Random order can skip dependencies or deadlines, so keep deadline-bound work out of the jar.
