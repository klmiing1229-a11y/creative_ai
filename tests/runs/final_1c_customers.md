**Problem:** Get 10 paying customers for a short video service aimed at small shops, using about 10 hours a week and HKD 500.
**Limits I used:** about 10 free hours a week, HKD 500 total, you are a student with a phone-level kit. I assumed you are in Hong Kong and can walk to shops.

## Obvious answers
What most AI replies would say. Listed on purpose; some may be exactly right.
1. Make 2-3 free sample videos for friendly shops to build a portfolio. Still right if you have nothing to show yet.
2. Cold DM shop owners on Instagram, Xiaohongshu or WhatsApp with a portfolio link. Still right for shops with active accounts.
3. Walk in and pitch owners face to face. Still right because Hong Kong shops are dense and owners are on site.
4. Run a launch discount for the first customers. Still right while your price is unproven.
5. Sell fixed packages (for example 3 reels for HKD X). Still right because owners dislike open-ended quotes.
6. Ask each happy customer for a testimonial and a referral. Still right once you have 2-3 customers.
7. Post your own work consistently so inbound leads find you. Still right as a slow background channel.
8. Join local business groups and uni networks and offer help. Still right for warm introductions.

## Hidden assumptions
- Each shop is sold to one at a time, as a custom job.
- The owner must be persuaded, so you need a portfolio or sample first.
- Any small shop is an equally good prospect.
- The purchase is a "video project", a considered decision with a deadline and follow-up.
- Your scarce resource is money, so you spend the HKD 500 on ads, gear or tools.

## Answers out of the box
### 1. Stretch: Hunt shops in their opening fortnight
**Idea:** Only pitch shops that are about to open or opened in the last 2 weeks. Look for "coming soon" signs on shutters and new "grand opening" posts. They have a deadline and a launch-content need, so you don't have to create demand. Offer a "grand opening clip" with a fixed delivery date.
**Move used:** Make the problem disappear. The persuasion problem goes away when the need already exists.
**Assumption broken:** Any small shop is an equally good prospect.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #3, and **how this differs:** you choose who to approach by a timing trigger, not by walking in on everyone.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Walk two districts for 3 hours, list 15 new or opening shops, and pitch all 15. Cost: fares, about HKD 50.
**Biggest risk:** Opening shops are busy and may not have a budget left; I don't know how many open per week near you.

### 2. Leap: Run a "Video Day" on one street
**Idea:** Take the school-photographer model. Pick one street or arcade and announce one Saturday with 10 slots of 20 minutes each. Each shop pays a fixed HKD 300 deposit by FPS to hold a slot, and you shoot all of them in one afternoon. Neighbouring shops see each other signing up, so they do your selling.
**Move used:** Borrow from a distant field (school picture day). Batching and a fixed date replace one-by-one custom sales.
**Assumption broken:** Each shop is sold to one at a time, as a custom job.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #5, and **how this differs:** the product is a dated event on one street with neighbours as social proof, not a package you pitch separately to each shop.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Print 20 flyers (about HKD 60), hand them to 20 shops on one street, and see whether 3 pay a deposit to hold a slot.
**Biggest risk:** Owners may not trust a stranger with no track record to turn up on a set day, and I don't know your deposit norms.

### 3. Weird but testable: Sell a HKD 80 ten-second clip at the counter
**Idea:** Walk in and say: "I'll film your best-selling item right now, 10 seconds, ready to post before I leave. HKD 80." There is no sample, no deliberation and no follow-up. Payment happens in the same visit, and the shot is finished in 10 minutes. Upsell to full packages only after they see the result.
**Move used:** Add an absurd constraint (10 minutes, on the spot). It removes the portfolio, the pitch and the quote.
**Assumption broken:** The owner must be persuaded first, and the purchase is a considered project.
**Nearest obvious answer:** #4, and **how this differs:** it is not a discount on a big job. It is a tiny finished product bought on impulse, with no sample, delay or follow-up.
**Cheapest test (under one week):** Do one afternoon of cold walk-ins to 15 shops. Cost: fares only. A win is 3 or more yes answers.
**Biggest risk:** HKD 80 may signal "cheap" and cap what these shops later pay you.
