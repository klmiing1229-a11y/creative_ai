## Step 0: Find where the waste comes from

The right fix depends on which of these four types you have:

| Type | Typical cause | Main fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Spoilage** (rotted or expired in storage) | Over-ordering, no stock rotation | Order smaller and more often, label everything |
| **Prep surplus** (cooked or cut, never sold) | Prepping for a busy day that didn't happen | Prep to a daily forecast, in two batches |
| **Plate waste** (customers leave it) | Portions too big, rice and sides unwanted | Smaller portions, choice of size |
| **Trim** (peels, offcuts) | Unused edible parts | Stock, staff meals, specials |

**Cost: about HK$30.** For 10 days, keep a notebook or taped-up sheet by the bin. Each night, write down what went in the bin, roughly how much, and which of the four types it was. Estimate its value in HK$. You will probably find that 3 or 4 items cause most of the loss, so you can focus on those.

## Ideas, roughly by payoff

1. **Prep to a forecast.** Use your 10-day log to set a "par" for each prepped item by weekday, for example "Tuesday: 8 portions of braised beef". Prep about 70% up front and top up during service. This is usually the biggest saver.
2. **Order smaller and more often.** Switch perishables (vegetables, seafood, herbs) to 2–3 smaller orders a week. A wet market or supplier near you may allow this. Slightly higher unit prices are usually cheaper than binning 20% of the stock.
3. **Label and rotate (FIFO).** Use masking tape and a marker to write the date on everything in the fridge. Put the oldest stock at the front, and give one shelf to "use first" items.
4. **Run a "use-it-first" special.** Whatever is closest to expiry goes into a daily special. Soups, congee, fried rice, stir-fries, curries and fillings all take odd leftovers well. Write the special on a small board.
5. **Cut or merge dishes that spoil.** If a menu item needs an ingredient that nothing else uses and it keeps rotting, drop it. Or rewrite another dish to share the ingredient. Aim for each fresh ingredient to appear in at least 2–3 dishes.
6. **Offer portion choices.** Try "half rice / less rice", a small size for single diners, and "tell us if you don't want the side". Look at what comes back on plates, since it's usually the same one or two items.
7. **Sell surplus at the end of the day.** In the last hour, offer discounted takeaway boxes to your regulars through a WhatsApp group or your door. Keep a simple regulars list and it costs nothing.
8. **Use trim.** Bones, shells, and vegetable and herb offcuts make stock. Edible trim can go into staff meals, which also cuts your staff food spend.
9. **Freeze before it spoils.** Use the freezer you already have. Portion and label items on day 2 or 3, not day 5.
10. **Give staff a reason to care.** If waste drops, put HK$50–100 a month toward a team meal or bonus. Staff see the waste before you do.

## Four-week plan

**Week 1: Measure.**
- Start the waste log and put date labels on everything in the fridge.
- Don't change anything else yet.

**Week 2: Quick wins.**
- Review the log and pick your top 3 wasted items.
- Set par levels for prepped items by weekday.
- Start the daily "use-it-first" special.
- Brief staff on what you are doing and why.

**Week 3: Ordering and portions.**
- Move 2–3 perishables to smaller, more frequent orders.
- Trial less-rice or half-portion options.
- Start the end-of-day surplus offer to regulars.

**Week 4: Review.**
- Compare the week 4 log to week 1 in HK$ of waste, and decide what stays.
- Cut or rework one dish that keeps causing spoilage.
- Set a target for month 2, for example 30% less waste by value.

## Things to check

- **Surplus donation.** Hong Kong has charities that take surplus food, such as Food Angel and Feeding Hong Kong. Their rules for restaurants (food safety, pickup areas) change, so call them for current terms.
- **Waste charging.** Hong Kong's municipal solid waste charging may affect your bin costs. Check the current status and how it applies to you, because it could add to what you save.
